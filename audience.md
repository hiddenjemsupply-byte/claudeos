# Audience: Iain Simons

Status: early. The brand is not locked in yet. This file covers the clothing brand idea and gets updated as the business takes shape.

## Who They Are
People into skateboarding, athletics, and fitness. They want clothes that are comfortable, look cool, and don't cost a fortune, because clothes nowadays are expensive.

They're active. They skate, train, play sports. They wear their clothes hard and need them to hold up.

They skew young and price aware. In an April 2026 CardRates survey, 47% of Gen Z said they'd bought a dupe or knockoff in the past year, the highest of any generation. ThredUp's 2026 Resale Report found secondhand clothing growing about twice as fast as new.

## The Problem I Solve
Affordability, cool designs, and comfort. Most brands make them pick two.

## What I Sell
Clothing. Price is not set yet, but it'll be on the cheaper end with really good material.

## Where They Hang Out
Instagram, TikTok, YouTube.

## Pain Points
What they're actually saying, from reviews and discussions I found:

* **Paying premium prices for basic quality.** One Gymshark reviewer said their logo started peeling off much sooner than they expected for a "premium" product. Others on the same review page called the products "ridiculously overpriced" and "poor quality."
* **Prices up, quality down.** A Gen Z respondent in a Who What Wear survey said prices have climbed while quality has dropped.
* **Gym clothes cost too much for what they are.** A Team Blind thread is titled "why are gym clothes so f*cking expensive," and people there blame brand markup and point each other to Costco, Walmart, and Amazon basics.
* **Markup on printed blanks.** A plain Gildan blank hoodie can sell for about $13. Print on demand hoodies are listed for $88 to $128. Buyers notice this.
* **Thin fabric.** Even at the high end, one comment compared brands and said Alo pieces are thinner than Lululemon's.

## Fears
* Getting burned on something that looks good online and falls apart after a few washes
* Paying brand prices for a logo instead of the clothes
* Going cheap and getting what they paid for. "You get what you pay for" comes up again and again in budget gear roundups.

## Dreams and Goals
* Clothes that feel good to train and skate in, and still look good out
* Cheap that doesn't feel cheap. Roundups of budget gear lean on exactly this idea, and one tester who bought 27 pairs of leggings found the best pair cost $23 and held up through rough washing.
* Standing out. One skate writer said skateboarding has no real uniform, so skaters are "obsessed with clothing." The look matters as much as the price.

## Language That Shows Up
* "overpriced" / "ridiculously overpriced"
* "for the price"
* "premium" (usually said with doubt)
* "holds up" / "held up through washing"
* "you get what you pay for"
* "dupe"
* "thin"
* "peeling" / "cracking" (logos and prints)

## How to Talk to Them
* Lead with real value: good material, low price, designs worth wearing
* Prove it holds up. Show washes, wear, skating and training in it. They've been burned before.
* Never call it "premium" unless I can prove it. They don't trust the word.
* Be straight about pricing and why it's low
